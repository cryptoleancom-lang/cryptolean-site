// Methodology · calib

Reliability buckets: what the market said, against what happened.

Current definition calib-v1. Charts stamped with it were computed by src/metrics/implied.py, and every value plotted for a past date could have been computed on that date.

Definition and failure modes

One observation per market-day: every daily quote on a contract that has since settled, bucketed by the probability it carried that day, against that contract's realised outcome. A well-calibrated venue puts 30% on things that happen 30% of the time.

Both counts are returned and both belong on the chart. n_days is the observation count; n_markets is how many independent events those days came from, and it is the smaller, more honest number — 200 days of quotes on one contract is one outcome observed 200 times, not 200 outcomes. Nothing here computes a confidence interval, because the interval that would be correct for n_markets and the one that would be correct for n_days differ by more than the result does.

min_days_before drops the final days before settlement, where the answer is often already known and calibration is trivially good.
