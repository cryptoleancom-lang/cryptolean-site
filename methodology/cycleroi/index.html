// Methodology · cycleroi

Cycle-anchored ROI, and every variant its controls switch between.

Current definition cycleroi-v2. Charts stamped with it were computed by src/metrics/cycleroi.py, and every value plotted for a past date could have been computed on that date.

Definition and failure modes

SPEC.md § 3 (2026-08-07): the cycle-ROI controls are series switches. The payload ships every variant, keyed; the browser picks one and computes nothing except the cohort mean and sigma. This module produces that grid.

x is DAYS SINCE ANCHOR, not a date. Day 0 is each cycle's own bottom or peak, which is the only thing that makes two cycles ninety years apart comparable.

How anchors are dated here, and how that differs from a common incumbent rule. The incumbent rule defines a cycle bottom as the lowest price while below its fair-value regression line. We do not use that rule, and the reason is worth stating on the chart rather than burying:

- Our fair value is an EXPANDING fit (`logreg-v1`), because a full-sample fit
  draws 2013's fair value using 2026 data. Applied to the expanding line, the
  below-the-line rule finds four episodes on Bitcoin and misses the 2018 and
  2022 bottoms entirely — the line has drifted under price by then.
- Applied to a full-sample fit it does reproduce all four known bottoms, and
  also admits five mid-cycle dips (2011-04, 2013-07, 2019-12, 2020-03,
  2020-09) that nobody calls cycle bottoms.

So anchors here are dated by ALTERNATION instead: a peak is a record high followed by a fall of at least MIN_FALL, and a bottom is the lowest close between one such peak and the next. On Bitcoin that reproduces 2011-11-22, 2015-01-15, 2018-12-16 and 2022-11-22 — the consensus dates — using only price and no fitted line.

It is still hindsight. A bottom is only a bottom once the recovery has happened, so none of these charts could have been drawn at the time, and the current cycle's anchor is provisional until it is not. Every series says which.
